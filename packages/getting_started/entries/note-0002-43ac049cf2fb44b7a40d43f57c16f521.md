## What Is This Handbook, and How Should You Use It?

**This is not Cartenza's internal design log.** That material is the raw record — every addendum, every course-correction, valuable for understanding *why* Cartenza's architecture is shaped the way it is, but not meant to orient someone new. This handbook is the curated, groomed counterpart: role and behavior content, starting from what Cousin (Claude Code's own working instance) already maintains for himself, generalized for anyone stepping into Cartenza work.

**Three different ways to use this handbook, depending on who you are — deliberately, not by accident:**

- **If you're an agent (a fresh Claude session, ChatGPT, or anything else) doing due diligence before engaging with a Cartenza-connected repo:** nothing here is gated behind a maturity signal — completeness serves you, staging doesn't apply. If you can read the whole handbook in one pass, do. **If your access is paginated or search-based, the minimum is this package (`getting_started`) plus whichever level covers the task in front of you** — that is enough to work correctly, and you can come back for the rest.
- **If you're a human new to Cartenza:** don't feel obligated to read this cover to cover first. The intended experience is closer to Claude Code's own onboarding — explore as questions come up, rather than memorize a long document upfront. Start with "Which MCP Server Do I Use?" (this package) if you're actively working, or the `log_book` package if you want the conceptual starting point.
- **If you're a curious, advanced user who wants to see everything laid out on demand:** it's all here — nothing is hidden from someone who goes looking, even if the default experience for a brand-new user is lighter-touch.

**The five packages below this one — `log_book`, `knowledge_packages`, `routines`, `playbooks`, `projects` — are Cartenza's own "staircase":** each one exists because solving the previous constraint revealed the next. They're ordered deliberately; reading them in order tells the real story of why Cartenza is shaped the way it is, not just what each piece does.

| Level | The constraint it solves |
|---|---|
| 1 — `log_book` | Conversations and context get forgotten between sessions |
| 2 — `knowledge_packages` | Captured information is hard to find and reuse once it accumulates |
| 3 — `routines` | Successful workflows are tedious to repeat by hand |
| 4 — `playbooks` | Coordinating multiple people and agents around shared ways of working is hard |
| 5 — `projects` | Good practices stay local instead of compounding across an organization |

**Not yet built into this handbook (by design, this stage):** a mechanism that watches your own behavior and proactively suggests the matching section just as you need it, rather than you having to browse here yourself. That's planned, but the detection logic lives in system-prompt/instructions layers, not in this repo — this repo is content only, for now, deliberately kept as a plain, inspectable git repo rather than hidden server-side, a tradeoff accepted deliberately at this stage.

**Reference:** Cartenza's internal design history (the full staging plan, just-in-time mechanism design, audience differentiation and governance considerations, and the original staircase reasoning in full).
