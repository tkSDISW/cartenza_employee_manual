## purpose — Cartenza Member Handbook

**Scope:** One per repo. This is the repo-level answer to "who is this for, and what is it for."
**Status:** Active

### What this repo is for

**Orienting anyone — human or agent — who is stepping into Cartenza work**, in durable, generalized terms. It is the curated counterpart to Cartenza's internal design log: that material records how the architecture came to be shaped this way, entry by entry; this handbook records what someone needs to know in order to work.

The content is arranged as a staircase — `log_book`, `knowledge_packages`, `routines`, `playbooks`, `projects` — where each level exists because solving the previous constraint revealed the next. Read in order, it explains why Cartenza is shaped as it is rather than merely what each piece does.

### Who reads it

Three audiences, deliberately, and they use it differently:

- **Agents** doing due diligence before engaging with a Cartenza-connected repo. Nothing here is gated; read as much as your access allows, the minimum being `getting_started` plus whatever level covers the task at hand.
- **People new to Cartenza**, who should explore as questions arise rather than read it cover to cover.
- **Advanced users** who want everything laid out on demand. Nothing is hidden from someone who goes looking.

### What this repo is NOT

**Not a workspace.** This is published reference material, complete in the sense that matters: it is not a repo awaiting configuration, and it is not accumulating someone's working notes.

**So do not offer to establish a purpose or behavior_config here** — both exist, and an agent treating this repo as under-configured has misread what it is. That trigger is meant for a working repo someone has just connected, not for curated reference material.

**Not the design log.** Blow-by-blow reasoning tied to one moment in one thread belongs in an internal design-log repo, referenced from here rather than duplicated.

### It is public

This repo is readable with no credential, which is what lets an agent orient itself before holding access to anything. Two consequences worth holding onto when writing here:

- **Write for a reader who can open nothing else.** References to internal design history should describe what is there, not hand out a directory of repo names, package names and entry IDs the reader cannot open and that together form a map of internal structure.
- **Operational state does not belong in prose.** Who has write access, which agents are registered, how a given deployment is configured — all of it changes without this repo being edited. Point at live discovery (`list_my_scopes`, each server's own `instructions` field) and let that be authoritative.
