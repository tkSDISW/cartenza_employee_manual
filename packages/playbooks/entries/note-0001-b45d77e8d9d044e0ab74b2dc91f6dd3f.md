## Level 4 — Playbooks: Coordinate People and Agents Around Shared Ways of Working

**The constraint this solves:** multi-agent, multi-person engineering fragments easily. Once multiple AI sessions and human participants are all contributing useful work, coordination itself becomes the hard part — onboarding, shared intent, ownership, and context that everyone involved actually has, not just whoever happened to be in the original conversation.

**Cartenza's response:** playbooks, sitting above routines. A playbook communicates *how* work should be approached, surfaces the relevant knowledge, connects reusable routines together, and gives both humans and agents guidance they can actually act on — closer to an employee manual or an engineering standard that participates directly in the work, not a document that sits on a shelf unread. **This manual is itself a step toward that** — deliberately not yet the final form (see `getting_started`'s note on what's not built here), but a real, concrete instance rather than staying purely theoretical.

**Status as of this writing:** the playbook layer is the least mature of the five — genuinely under active design, not yet a proven, reused pattern the way the log book and knowledge packages are. If you're exploring this level, expect more open questions than settled answers.

**A concrete signal you're working at this level:** more than one person or agent needs to work consistently against the same knowledge and the same expectations, and informal "just ask whoever did it last time" coordination has started breaking down.
