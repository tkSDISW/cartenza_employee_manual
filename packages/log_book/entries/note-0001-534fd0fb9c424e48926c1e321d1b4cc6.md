## Level 1 — The Log Book: Don't Lose What Already Happened

**The constraint this solves:** conversations and context get forgotten. AI-assisted work produces real reasoning, decisions, diagnostics, and observations — but that knowledge is usually trapped inside one chat session or model. Starting a new session often means re-explaining everything from scratch, and the human ends up as the bottleneck: the one who has to stop and reconstruct what already happened.

**Cartenza's response:** a persistent log book. Capture what happened, when, and why — outside the chat itself, in git. That makes the record portable, versioned, inspectable, and independent of whichever AI provider or session produced it.

**What this looks like in practice, in this system:** `knowledge_repo`'s `add_log_entry`/`append_log_entry` tools. Four entry types — `observation`, `decision`, `lesson_learned`, `routine_def` — plus freer note/milestone/issue framing layered on top. Every entry lives as its own file, in git, in a named package. See `getting_started`/"Which MCP Server Do I Use?" if you haven't already — this is the layer `knowledge_repo` owns.

**Why this is the foundation, not just one feature among several:** every layer above this one — Knowledge Packages, Routines, Playbooks, Projects — depends on there being a durable, queryable record underneath it. Nothing above this staircase level works if the raw material (what actually happened, and why) isn't captured somewhere real in the first place.

**A concrete signal you're working at this level, and that's fine:** you're logging observations and decisions as you go, one entry at a time, without yet needing to search across many entries or repeat a workflow. That's the normal, healthy starting point — not a sign you're behind.
